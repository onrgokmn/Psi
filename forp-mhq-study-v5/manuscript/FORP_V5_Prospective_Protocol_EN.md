# Fear of Relapse and Subsequent Psychological Well-Being in Psychiatric Outpatients: A Prespecified Prospective Cohort With Separate Turkish Adaptation and Cultural Inquiry

## Manuscript-ready study protocol · FORP-TR V5.0 · 31 October 2026

**Article type:** Observational cohort study protocol / planned mixed-methods investigation.  
**Registration:** OSF registration prepared, *not yet submitted*.  
**Ethics and instrument permissions:** Pending.  
**Clinical results:** None available. A previous generated dataset is excluded from all clinical Results and Abstract claims; its sole purpose is Monte Carlo design evaluation in Supplementary Appendix A.

## Structured abstract

**Background:** Fears of recurrence or progression (FORP) may persist after acute psychiatric symptoms improve, but it remains uncertain whether they provide prospective information about well-being beyond baseline symptom severity, illness history and current care. Transdiagnostic measurement is particularly vulnerable to semantic and cultural variation in the meaning of relapse, family responsibility and disclosure of psychiatric illness.

**Objective:** To assess the prespecified conditional association between baseline FORP-MHQ severity and six-month WHO-5 psychological well-being in adult psychiatric outpatients, adjusting for baseline clinical and demographic covariates. Secondary objectives are to evaluate nonlinearity, document diagnosis-specific clinical deterioration with blinded adjudication, and contextualize quantitative patterns in purposively sampled interviews.

**Methods:** A separate translation and cognitive-debriefing phase (15–20 interviews) and feasibility pilot (30–40 participants) will precede recruitment. These participants will not enter the primary cohort analysis. A cohort of 700 outpatients will be recruited, with approximately 560 six-month assessments expected if attrition is 20%. Baseline questionnaires will comprise FORP-MHQ (10 items), PHQ-9 (9), GAD-7 (7), WHO-5 (5), the MSPSS family-support subscale (4), and two nonvalidated context questions (37 responses in total). The primary outcome is WHO-5 at six months. The primary prespecified regression will adjust for baseline WHO-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7, age, sex, diagnostic category, log-transformed illness duration, past episode burden, baseline treatment category, remission status and centre if applicable. The coefficient per ten FORP-MHQ points will be estimated using HC3-robust inference. A three-knot restricted cubic spline analysis will examine nonlinearity as a prespecified secondary analysis. Clinical events will be independently adjudicated by two psychiatrists blinded to FORP and cultural-response data. Embedded interviews will explore family support, autonomy and disclosure concerns.

**Sample-size basis:** A reproducible Monte Carlo framework incorporating the covariate structure and planned attrition was used to select a recruitment target of 700. Numerical simulation outcomes and their uncertainty are reported solely in Supplementary Appendix A. No design assumption is presented as a clinical result.

**Discussion:** The design tests incremental prospective association without making causal claims. Separating the translation pilot from outcome analysis, accounting for illness history and care, and predefining event adjudication should strengthen interpretability. Generalizability, measurement equivalence, nonrandom loss to follow-up and limited power for interactions remain material limitations.

**Keywords:** fear of recurrence; relapse; patient-reported outcomes; psychiatry; well-being; Turkish adaptation; prospective cohort; mixed methods; preregistration.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1. Why the clinical question matters

For people living with mental disorders, recovery is not reducible to the absence of an acute episode. The prospect of relapse may be relevant even during clinical stability: it may influence vigilance toward symptoms, treatment attendance, avoidance of valued activities and decisions about disclosure of illness. Importantly, a fear-related concern that appears adaptively vigilant in one context may co-occur with restrictive behavior in another. The direction of these associations cannot be inferred from a single cross-sectional scale score.

Coutts-Bain and colleagues developed the ten-item Fears of Recurrence and Progression about Mental Health Questionnaire (FORP-MHQ) in a sample of 865 respondents with diverse psychiatric conditions. The original work provides useful evidence for a transdiagnostic single-factor measure, while a subsequent study addressed a broader conceptual model [1,2]. These studies do not resolve the specific question addressed here: whether baseline recurrence fears provide incremental information about patient-reported well-being six months later after accounting for symptom burden, illness trajectory, treatment and remission.

## 1.2. Why cross-cultural adaptation is not merely translation

A participant may interpret 'becoming unwell again' as a return of symptoms, loss of independence, strain on family relationships, loss of work or unwanted disclosure. The instrument's wording requires cognitive interviewing and explicit cultural relevance checks, not only back-translation or favorable reliability coefficients. The study therefore assigns separate roles to (i) pre-cohort linguistic and content adaptation, (ii) outcome-blinded measurement-property analysis at baseline, and (iii) longitudinal clinical inference. The same cohort may contribute baseline data to psychometrics and subsequent well-being analysis, a source of dependence that will be disclosed rather than represented as independent external validation [1,3].

## 1.3. A specific, falsifiable question

The primary analysis concerns a *conditional association*, not the causal effect of fear or a recurrence prediction instrument. The single primary coefficient is the adjusted mean difference in six-month WHO-5 per ten-point higher baseline FORP-MHQ score. All additional models, especially effect modification and diagnosis-specific outcome contrasts, are secondary or exploratory. The distinct possibility of nonlinear, potentially U-shaped, associations is planned in advance rather than selected after viewing the data.

# 2. Methods

## 2.1. Study setting, registration and governance

This proposed six-month, observational, transdiagnostic cohort will be coordinated in an outpatient psychiatry setting in Balıkesir, Türkiye. A second centre may be activated for recruitment feasibility before any six-month outcomes are accessed, subject to site-specific approval. The protocol, full statistical analysis plan, measurement version, codebook and power-simulation assumptions will be deposited in an immutable OSF registration prior to the first cohort recruit. As of this version, no registration, institutional ethics approval or author permission has been obtained; no participant data have been collected. Deviations from the locked protocol will be dated, explained and distinguished from prespecified analyses. The report will follow STROBE, with a modular COSMIN 2.0 assessment for measurement properties [3,4].

## 2.2. Participants and sampling

Consecutive eligible adults (aged ≥18 years) with documented lived experience of a mental disorder will be approached during routine follow-up. Clinical diagnoses and their supporting records are required, but no major diagnostic family will be excluded solely by diagnosis. Acute delirium, impaired consent capacity, severe cognitive or communication obstacles to meaningful questionnaire completion, or circumstances in which research would interfere with urgent care will result in postponement or non-enrolment. Participant screening, reasons for exclusion and refusal, and follow-up status will be counted prospectively. Repeat visits will not create repeat enrolment records.

## 2.3. Distinct measurement adaptation and pilot phase

Before the cohort begins, written permission will be obtained for translation and research use of FORP-MHQ. Two independent forward translations, reconciliation, two blinded back-translations and a clinical/linguistic expert review will be followed by 15–20 cognitive interviews and a separate 30–40-person pilot. Relevance, comprehensibility, cultural connotations, response-category discrimination and completion time will be reviewed. Pilot respondents will not contribute to the primary cohort dataset. The final Turkish wording and scoring guide will be locked before the first cohort baseline assessment.

Baseline FORP item responses in the enrolled cohort will subsequently be assessed with a one-factor ordinal confirmatory factor model using WLSMV and complementary indices, item thresholds and standardized loadings. Reliability indices will only be interpreted in light of the dimensionality findings. Test–retest agreement will be assessed 21–35 days later in approximately 70–90 clinically stable participants with prespecified ICC and measurement-error methods. The psychometric analysts will not receive six-month outcomes until the instrument report has been signed off. This is a temporal analysis firewall, **not** an external independent validation sample.

## 2.4. Questionnaire selection and patient burden

Baseline responses comprise FORP-MHQ (10 items), WHO-5 (5 items), PHQ-9 (9 items), GAD-7 (7 items), perceived family support (MSPSS family subscale, four items) and two locally written context questions. Thus the baseline task consists of 37 responses, with the objective of remaining within 12 minutes, to be verified in the pilot. The sole scheduled repeated patient instrument at months three and six is the five-item WHO-5. Expanded PHQ-9 and GAD-7 symptom measures replace the four-item PHQ-4 to improve characterization of potentially confounding depressive and anxiety symptom burden [5,6]. Responding to the PHQ-9 self-harm item will activate a predefined clinical safety assessment pathway, not automatic risk diagnosis. Copyright and validated Turkish versions will be verified before use.

## 2.5. Prespecified baseline clinical covariates

The following variables are collected at baseline from routine clinical assessment or documented records: age; self-reported sex/gender variable(s) with coding rules locked before outcome access; diagnostic family (depression, anxiety/OCD/trauma-related, bipolar/psychotic spectrum, other); illness duration in years (modeled as log[1+years]); prior episode burden (0, 1, ≥2, or unknown); baseline treatment category (no pharmacotherapy; medication alone; medication with structured psychotherapy; psychotherapy-only recorded descriptively); baseline clinical remission (documented yes/no/uncertain); and centre if more than one participates. The reference category and approach to sparse categories will be fixed before outcome unblinding. Post-baseline treatment changes or intermediate WHO-5 values will not be adjusted for in the primary model because they could reflect downstream processes and introduce bias.

## 2.6. Outcomes and blinded diagnosis-specific adjudication

The primary endpoint is the six-month WHO-5 total (0–100, higher is better). WHO-5 is a well-being measure, not a diagnostic relapse criterion. Secondary clinical events will be classified by principal diagnosis, distinguishing a new documented episode after remission/stabilization from worsening of an already active disorder. Depression requires clinician-confirmed new syndromal episode and appropriate duration following documented remission. Bipolar presentations require episode-specific criteria for mania, hypomania or depression; psychotic-spectrum episodes require renewed clinically significant symptoms after stabilization; chronic anxiety/OCD presentations distinguish recurrent syndromes from continuous symptom exacerbation. These operational definitions must be finalized and locked by the clinical panel before recruitment; hospitalization or medication modification alone does not establish a relapse.

Two independent psychiatrist adjudicators will receive dated medical records and relevant clinical information without access to baseline FORP, family support, disclosure concerns or research interview narratives. Disagreements will be settled by a third independent clinician, and uncertainty will be retained rather than forced into a binary event. Ratings cannot be fully blinded to all clinical history, and this limitation will be documented. Events will not be pooled as an interchangeable transdiagnostic relapse rate; insufficient event counts will preclude inferential survival analysis.

## 2.7. Sample-size design and Monte Carlo verification

The selected initial recruitment target is 700, corresponding to approximately 560 six-month endpoints with a mean 20% loss. Reproducible Monte Carlo simulations (seed 20261031, 500 replications per scenario) generate plausible but hypothetical correlations among FORP, WHO-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7, diagnostic family, illness duration, episode burden, remission and treatment. WHO-5 boundaries and four-point increments, heteroskedasticity-robust HC3 inference, and missingness dependent on observed baseline variables are explicitly represented. The size and interpretation of prospective associations depend on the assumed effect and attrition process; smaller effects may not be reliably detected. All numerical power scenarios, error and coverage estimates, and code are restricted to Supplementary Appendix A. None is an observed clinical outcome.

## 2.8. The single primary statistical model

The conditional WHO-5 mean at month six will be modeled by linear regression with the FORP-MHQ baseline total divided by ten, baseline WHO-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7, age, sex/gender variable, diagnostic-family indicators, log(1+illness duration), prior episode burden, baseline treatment category, remission status and centre when applicable. The primary inferential quantity is the FORP coefficient with HC3 95% confidence interval and two-sided p value. Reduced (without FORP) and full models will yield ΔR² and partial R², defined as ΔR²/(1−R²_reduced). Model diagnostics will examine influential observations, functional form, conditional heteroskedasticity, collinearity and bounded-outcome effects. No stepwise variable selection or post hoc cut-off optimization will be applied.

## 2.9. Prespecified secondary nonlinearity

A restricted cubic spline of baseline FORP-MHQ, with three knots fixed at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of the *baseline exposure distribution*, will be fitted with the same covariates as the primary model. A joint Wald test will examine its nonlinear component. Adjusted predictions and uncertainty bands will be plotted across the observed support, with no extrapolated extremes. A significant nonlinear test or visually curved fit does not itself establish a clinically meaningful U-shaped association. This secondary analysis will be interpreted as hypothesis-generating, and multiplicity will be discussed.

## 2.10. Missing data and robustness

Follow-up will be presented by separate baseline, month-three and month-six response patterns. Participants absent at month three but observed at month six remain eligible for the primary analysis. Complete-outcome regression is the prespecified primary analysis; robustness will be evaluated using multiple imputation under a MAR assumption, inverse-probability weighting with weight/positivity diagnostics, and pattern-mixture delta sensitivity under MNAR (missing six-month WHO-5 values shifted 5, 10 or 15 points below the MAR distribution). Deviations in estimated magnitude or sign across these scenarios will be reported. A single deterministic imputation will not be used to construct apparently precise inferential intervals.

## 2.11. Exploratory cultural component and qualitative integration

Approximately 24–30 volunteers will be purposively sampled across combinations of high/low baseline FORP and high/low perceived family support, aiming for six to eight interviews in each of four cells while allowing clinical and demographic diversity. Interview prompts address the meaning of recurrence, family care versus control, privacy, disclosure decisions, restricted life activities and contradictory experiences. A framework approach with reflexive notes, audit trail, discussion of counterexamples and a joint-display matrix will link the qualitative and quantitative findings without describing interview themes as causal proof. Qualitative subgroup differences will not be treated as representative population estimates [7,8].

## 2.12. Ethics, confidentiality and reporting

No recruitment or instrument deployment will begin before ethics and institutional permissions, licensing and final wording approval. Pseudonymous research data will be held in institution-controlled secure storage; the identifying linkage key is separate. Participants select safe contact methods. Audio recording requires explicit, separate consent. Real participant records or interviews will not be published on GitHub or in an unrestricted OSF repository. STROBE and COSMIN 2.0 compliance crosswalks, the prespecified event-adjudication guide and the registration field set accompany this protocol.

# 3. Planned reporting and interpretation (not clinical findings)

Upon completion, the primary manuscript will include a participant flow diagram, baseline characteristics and missingness, a standalone measurement-property report, prespecified main and secondary estimates with confidence intervals, MAR/MNAR sensitivity results, diagnosis-specific events and a qualitative joint display. The present document contains no clinical Results section, patient quotations, invented ethics numbers, or retrospective declarations of having recruited participants.

A negative association might support further research on recurrence fear as a clinical concern but would not demonstrate that reducing fear improves subsequent well-being. A null estimate with wide intervals could arise from weak effects, attrition, measurement error, confounding or diagnostic heterogeneity. A nonlinear association could reflect differences in severity, treatment or remitted versus active illness rather than a truly adaptive range of fear. The study is observational and may be one-centre, while the psychometric and prospective arms share baseline participants; these limitations must remain visible in any eventual submission.
